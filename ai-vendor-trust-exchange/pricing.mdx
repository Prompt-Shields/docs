## The value metric: per vendor monitored

Seat-based pricing under-monetizes a data product. Ten reviewers reading one profile is not ten times the value of one reviewer reading it — but one team keeping **two hundred** AI vendors current is genuinely two hundred times the work behind the scenes.

So the meter is **per vendor monitored**, which is where the third-party-risk and security-ratings market has converged for the same reason. It maps price directly to the headline value: keeping a defined set of AI vendors current.

The boundary between free and paid falls on **freshness**, not on features:

| | Free | Paid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **What it is** | A point-in-time trust profile | A continuously monitored one |
| **Includes** | Summary score, basic facts, [Ask ATX](/ai-vendor-trust-exchange/ask-atx), the option to claim | Full current due diligence, change alerts, freshness commitments, exports |
| **The catch** | Accurate on the day you read it, and decaying from then on | `last_verified_at` keeps advancing, and you are told when posture moves |

That split is deliberate. A static report is a good lead magnet and a poor product — because for due diligence, [currency is the whole point](/ai-vendor-trust-exchange/trust-profiles#freshness-is-a-product-surface). What is worth paying for is not the answer; it is the answer *staying true*.

## The tiers

| Tier | Who it is for | Includes | Indicative band |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Public trust profile** | Everyone | Point-in-time summary, basic facts, Ask ATX, claim option. No alerts, no freshness commitment. | **Free** |
| **Buyer — Starter** | SMB security and procurement | Full current DD plus monitoring for roughly 5–10 vendors, change alerts, exports | **~$5K–10K/yr**, self-serve |
| **Buyer — Pro** | Mid-market | A larger per-vendor bundle (roughly 25–50), faster alerts, questionnaire auto-fill, integrations | **~$20K–60K/yr**, per-vendor meter |
| **Buyer — Enterprise** | Enterprise TPRM and GRC | Large or unlimited coverage, SSO, a freshness SLA, EU AI Act and ISO 42001 mapping, API access, named support | **$75K–250K+/yr**, negotiated |
| **Vendor — verified profile** | Rated AI vendors | Claim, respond to findings, dispute workflow, keep your *own* data current, verified badge, buyer-intent leads | **~$5K–25K/vendor/yr**, tiered by reach |
| **API / data** | GRC platforms and programmatic consumers | Usage-metered access to current scores and change events | **Usage-based**, add-on |
| **Expert / deep assessment** | Buyers needing depth on one vendor | Human-led deep assessment, one-off or on retainer | **$5K–35K/engagement** |

Free membership also runs on [give-to-get](/ai-vendor-trust-exchange/exchange): contributing an assessment unlocks full reports without a subscription. Paying and contributing are two routes to the same depth, and the exchange needs both.

## What it displaces

The honest comparison is not against another subscription. It is against **a manual vendor security review — roughly $5K–35K per assessment, and stale on the day it lands**.

One such review costs about what a year of Starter does, covers one vendor instead of ten, and begins decaying immediately. That is the entire ROI argument, and it is worth stating plainly rather than dressing up: ATX is not cheaper than doing nothing, it is cheaper than doing the review you are already obliged to do.

<Note>
Two adjacent markets bracket the pricing. Third-party-risk and security-ratings tools price per vendor monitored and set the realistic entry point and meter. Dedicated AI-governance platforms price per model or per programme, an order of magnitude higher, and set the ceiling on what this job is worth — not the entry point. ATX deliberately sits on the lower meter.
</Note>

## Availability

The tiers do not all arrive together, and the sequencing is a consequence of the product rather than a sales plan:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Public profiles and vendor claiming — first">
    Vendor claiming is the earliest paid surface, because it is the one that works before the catalog is dense. It also seeds supply: a vendor keeping their own profile current makes the directory better for everyone reading it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Buyer monitoring tiers — once freshness is real">
    Starter and Pro stay dark until ratings and re-verification are demonstrably trustworthy and coverage in the beachhead vertical is credible. Selling monitoring before the monitoring works would spend the credibility the product runs on.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enterprise, API and expert engagements — last">
    These need coverage density, a stable methodology, and a vetted expert panel behind them.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Money never reaches a verdict

<Warning>
**No tier on this page buys score influence — including the vendor tier.** A vendor paying for a verified profile is buying presence, the right to respond, and the ability to keep their own data current. They are not buying a better rating, a higher [Ask ATX](/ai-vendor-trust-exchange/ask-atx) ranking, or a say in the methodology.
</Warning>

This is the part of the pricing model that is load-bearing rather than commercial. Vendors are monetized first, which is precisely the arrangement that makes buyers suspect a directory of being pay-to-play — so the separation cannot be a promise on a pricing page. It is enforced by separate services and isolation tests in CI, and published as a ratings-independence policy alongside the methodology.

It is also the proven pattern in this market: the credible incumbents monetize badges, lead generation and vendor-side tooling, and refuse to sell anything that touches the score. Independence is not a constraint on the business model here. It is the reason anyone would consult the directory instead of the vendor's own marketing — which makes it the business model.
